The problem

Impersonation signals are scattered.

Lookalike domains, suspicious social identities, app listings, and screenshots often arrive through separate channels. Analysts need to connect those signals without losing where each observation came from—or treating an unverified match as a fact.

SentinelX brings submissions and configured evidence sources into a reviewable workflow with explicit provider status, rule explanations, and case history.

What SentinelX does

One investigation, with its evidence attached.

Use the capabilities supported by the current application. External-source availability depends on provider configuration.

⌕

Investigate candidates

Analyze URLs, domains, names, social handles, app identifiers, and submitted screenshots through the available evidence pipeline.

◎

Compare brand identity

Use registered brand assets to distinguish verified official matches, uncertain identities, and candidates that continue to review.

≋

Explain risk signals

Apply deterministic, explainable rules and optional model scoring. Scores prioritize review; they are not proof or calibrated probabilities.

▧

Read screenshot evidence

Validate and decode uploaded images. Optional local OCR and configured vision AI are kept as attributed observations, not identity verification.

↻

Track configured checks

Persist monitoring targets, check history, provider status, observations, and evidence-change alerts. A target is active only after a successful check.

▤

Keep a reviewable case

Persist cases, investigation snapshots, monitoring alert context, and analyst review history for follow-up.

Evidence transparency

Know what was observed—and what was not.

Evidence records preserve source and retrieval context. Grounded analysis validates citations against retrieved records. Search snippets remain snippets; an unavailable provider does not become a negative finding.

Limits are part of the result.
A heuristic similarity is not proof of impersonation. OCR or a vision model may misread an image. Search coverage can be incomplete. Social discovery and some store metadata require authorized provider access. Monitoring runs only as configured and is not a guarantee of continuous external coverage.
